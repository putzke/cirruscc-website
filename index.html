Features

Everything a PI team needs — compliance, reporting, and a live window for your client

Cirrus Cc is the consultant's own workspace — built for the daily PI workflow, FHWA/NEPA compliance documentation, and live reporting to the agency client. It complements public engagement platforms; it doesn't replace them.

Stakeholder registry

Master list plus per-project contact lists. Contacts reuse across projects. Role, support level, influence tier and report distribution tracked per project. Duplicate detection, 13 stakeholder types. Search by name, org, email, phone or parcel number.

Interaction logging

Log every contact — channel, direction, subject, nature, summary, who logged it. Anonymous callers are first-class records. Quick Log grid for batch entry. NEPA stage tagging on every entry. Every record is entered by a person; none are AI-generated.

Follow-ups and commitments

Follow-ups with due date, owner, overdue flags and resolution notes. Commitments to the public with 15+ categories, fulfillment tracking and date. An "Assigned to me" view on desktop and mobile. Overdue items flagged on the dashboard.

Issues tracker

Category, priority, status, linked interactions and resolution summary. Export a single issue or a full issues summary as a Word document. Issues flow into every PI report automatically.

Deliverables and events

Contracted deliverable quantities with a progress bar and delivered counter. Milestone, recurring-cadence and fixed-quantity types. Events and meetings with attendees, equity toggles and action-item notes.

FHWA / NEPA compliance

NEPA classification and stage on every project. Classification-specific checklists — 33 items for a CE, 49 for an EA — with UDOT and CFR citations. Comment periods, hearing notice timing check against Utah Admin Code R930-2-5, and a NEPA Compliance report.

LEP / Environmental Justice

Limited English Proficiency and underserved / EJ population flags on every contact. Filter, report and count them. Equity form tracking for public events. Counts surface in every PI compliance report — supporting Title VI and EO 12898 documentation.

Map and geospatial tools

Contacts and parcels on one map, colored by acquisition status. Draw any shape — corridor, neighborhood, school zone — and see every stakeholder and parcel inside it, with LEP and EJ counts called out. Check parcels against Utah's statewide UGRC layer. Canvassing placeholders for door-to-door outreach.

Right-of-way and parcel register

Many-to-many ownership — co-owners, heirs, LLCs. Acquisition type and status, notice dates, legal description and design exhibit tracking. Outreach tracking per owner contact with authority-to-sign flags and sentiment. Excel export: parcel register, mailing list and agent briefing sheets.

PI report editor

Pick and order sections, set the reporting period, and see a live preview as you edit. AI drafts each section — or all at once — from facts the app computes. The AI writes the prose; it never counts. Export to Word with Sunrise, Sunrise Alt or UDOT letterhead.

Report archive and status report

Each archived report is frozen as issued. Attach the final hand-edited Word file as the report of record for client download. The AI Project Status Report compares current pace, commitments, issues and engagement against the last report.

PI close-out report

The final deliverable to the client. Guided intake asks only what the app can't compute. AI drafts the PI highlights and the Stakeholder Communications Log Summary. Computed charts — contacts per month, channels, topics, issues escalated — print in black and white. Word export with real hyperlinks and letterhead.

Mobile field app

Log interactions, look up contacts, call or email them, and check assigned follow-ups — on any smartphone, no install required. Canvassing support with placeholder contacts ready to confirm in the field. Parcel details and status visible on site.

Stakeholder importer

Import spreadsheets of contacts, interactions and parcels. Columns map automatically, every row previews before anything is written, duplicates are skipped. Refuses to run if the database connection drops — a disconnection can never create duplicate records.

AI contact import

Paste a sign-in sheet, an email block or a list — or attach a photo or PDF — and AI extracts contacts into a review grid. Nothing is saved until a person approves it. Contacts only; interaction records are never AI-generated.

How it works

From first contact to FHWA-ready report

01

Set up your project

Create a project, set the NEPA classification (CE / EA / EIS), and import your contact roster in minutes. Duplicate detection and auto-assigned report distribution groups keep your registry clean from day one.

02

Log every interaction — with NEPA context

Desktop or mobile — your team logs calls, emails, meetings, and field contacts as they happen. Every interaction is tagged to a NEPA process stage, a stakeholder, and a project. LEP and Environmental Justice flags are built into every contact record.

03

Track compliance, issues, and commitments

Log public comments with comment periods and response tracking. Record commitments across 15+ categories. Flag concerns as issues, tag them by category and priority, and track them to resolution — all in one place.

04

Draft your report with AI

Open the PI Report Editor and let AI draft your narrative sections from logged data — concerns, outreach summary, NEPA compliance status, trends. Review, edit, and refine. Export a branded Word document ready to submit.

05

Give your client a live window — not a quarterly PDF

The PI Client Portal gives your DOT, city, or county client a secure, read-only view of public engagement activity and deliverable progress on their specific project — updated in real time as your team logs work. No other PI platform offers this. Your client sees what they need; your internal workflow stays private.

Meet the founder

A career built on telling complex stories simply

Jeff Putzke
Founder / CEO
LinkedIn →

Cirrus Cc exists because Jeff Putzke has lived every side of public communication — and saw firsthand how much of it still runs on spreadsheets, sticky notes, and tribal knowledge. His career started not in stakeholder engagement, but in code. As a computer programmer for St. Paul Regional Water Services in the mid-1990s, Jeff learned how to design systems that solve real, repeatable problems. That foundation in software thinking never left him.

From there, his path took an unusual turn. Armed with a Bachelor of Science in Meteorology from St. Cloud State University, Jeff spent the next 15+ years as a broadcast meteorologist across six major television markets — Minneapolis, Omaha, Traverse City, Salt Lake City (KTVX ABC4), Tucson (Chief Meteorologist, KGUN 9), and Sacramento (KOVR/CBS) — and continued as a freelance meteorologist in the Salt Lake market through 2022. Live television taught him what an audience needs: clear narratives, plain language, and the ability to distill complex technical information into something a viewer cares about in under thirty seconds. Mass engagement, public speaking, and storytelling design became second nature.

Between broadcasting and his current role, Jeff spent several years as a sales and design engineer for a SaaS clinical research software company, where he learned the discipline of translating user needs into software features, scoping product requirements with stakeholders, and selling technical solutions to non-technical buyers. That experience would later prove foundational to the way Cirrus Cc was designed — built from the inside out, by someone who has sat on both sides of the demo.

In 2019, Jeff joined Sunrise Engineering and built the Public Involvement practice from a one-person specialty into a full team serving UDOT, municipal, and utility projects across Utah. Along the way, he worked on dozens of high-profile transportation and infrastructure communications efforts. Cirrus Cc is the natural product of that arc — software engineering instincts, SaaS product sensibility, broadcast-honed clarity, and a deep understanding of what PI teams actually do every day, built into one tool by someone who has lived the work.
